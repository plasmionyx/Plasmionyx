PLASMIONYX

A Public-Interest Initiative for Planetary Plasma Energy Research

Founding concept | October 2026
Explore the cosmos. Protect our home.

1. Mission

Plasmionyx is a proposed open research initiative investigating whether the plasma and electromagnetic environments of Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune could be harnessed to generate useful electrical power.

Our mission is to investigate bold possibilities with scientific rigor, transparent methods, and a commitment to the public good. We seek to understand what nature makes possible, what engineering could achieve, and whether any proposed system could deliver meaningful net energy.

We begin with a question, not a predetermined answer.

2. Long-Term Vision

We envision a civilization where clean, reliable, and increasingly abundant energy supports human flourishing, protects Earth’s ecosystems, and expands opportunity for everyone.

We believe scientific progress should improve life rather than deepen inequality. If a new energy technology proves viable, its development should prioritize environmental responsibility, affordability, transparency, and broad public benefit.

Plasmionyx’s long-term vision is to contribute to a future in which technological advancement helps humanity live in greater balance with Earth and explore the cosmos responsibly.

3. Scientific Objectives

Plasmionyx will pursue the following objectives:

1. Compare the four giant planets. Investigate the plasma, magnetic fields, charged-particle environments, and relevant energy flows around Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune.
2. Build on existing evidence. Analyze publicly available spacecraft observations, scientific literature, and established physical models.
3. Evaluate candidate technologies. Investigate electrodynamic tethers, electromagnetic induction, and other physically plausible energy-collection concepts.
4. Quantify feasibility. Estimate potential electrical output, collection efficiency, auxiliary power requirements, conversion losses, environmental constraints, and uncertainty.
5. Identify practical tests. Determine which small-scale simulations, calculations, or laboratory experiments could test the most important assumptions.
6. Develop an interdisciplinary community. Bring together plasma physicists, planetary scientists, engineers, programmers, energy researchers, and interested members of the public.
7. Assess real-world value. Compare any promising concept with alternative energy technologies and consider whether it could offer a meaningful environmental, scientific, or practical advantage.

4. Scientific Integrity

Plasmionyx will distinguish clearly between:

* Established physical principles and speculative hypotheses.
* Theoretical estimates and experimentally demonstrated results.
* Energy present in an environment and energy a device can actually extract.
* Gross electrical generation and net useful power after operating requirements and losses.
* Technical possibility and economic or operational viability.

We commit to publishing methods, assumptions, uncertainties, and results wherever possible. Negative results are valuable findings, not failures to conceal.

We welcome independent criticism and will not present unverified claims of unlimited, cost-free, or commercially viable energy as established fact. We will revise our conclusions when evidence warrants it.

5. Public-Interest Principles

Plasmionyx is guided by six principles:

Open science. Share data sources, calculations, models, and findings wherever licenses and legitimate safety or privacy considerations permit.

Independent scrutiny. Invite qualified researchers to challenge assumptions and evaluate results.

Environmental stewardship. Assess potential ecological consequences and the full life cycle of proposed technologies.

Equitable benefit. Consider affordability, accessibility, and the distribution of benefits from any successful development.

Transparent governance. Disclose funding sources, conflicts of interest, decision-making processes, and relevant limitations.

International cooperation. Encourage participation across disciplines, institutions, and borders.

No contributor or donor should be able to suppress unfavorable results or purchase control over the scientific conclusions.

6. Initial Research Plan

Our first milestone is a reproducible preliminary feasibility assessment of all four giant planets.

First 30 days: Establish the foundation

* Publish the project charter and collaborator invitation.
* Create an open research repository and research-question register.
* Inventory relevant public spacecraft data and scientific literature.
* Define shared modeling assumptions and standards for documenting uncertainty.
* Recruit an initial group of scientific and engineering collaborators.

Days 31–60: Develop preliminary models

* Compare the four planetary environments.
* Select candidate collection concepts for analysis.
* Estimate relevant voltages, currents, power flows, losses, and engineering requirements.
* Document radiation, thermal, mechanical, navigation, and maintenance constraints.
* Identify the assumptions with the greatest influence on feasibility.

Days 61–90: Test and review

* Publish preliminary calculations and reproducible code where possible.
* Invite independent technical review, including skeptical reviewers.
* Document uncertainties, missing data, and negative findings.
* Publish a feasibility assessment and a recommendation to continue, revise, or stop each research direction.

Progress will be measured by evidence and reproducibility, not by the boldness of our promises.

7. Participation and Resources

Plasmionyx welcomes contributions from plasma physicists, planetary scientists, electrical and aerospace engineers, scientific programmers, energy systems researchers, educators, students, and public-interest advocates.

The initial phase will emphasize open data, open-source tools, and volunteer collaboration. No financial contribution is required to participate.

We will seek specific, manageable contributions and respect the time and expertise of volunteers. Formal funding or institutional partnerships will be considered transparently as the project develops.

8. Accountability

Before pursuing hardware development or making claims about practical deployment, Plasmionyx will require documented evidence addressing:

* The physical source of the energy.
* The amount of energy accessible to a proposed collector.
* Predicted electrical output and the assumptions behind it.
* Auxiliary power consumption and conversion losses.
* Net power that could realistically be delivered.
* Mass, materials, lifetime, deployment, and maintenance requirements.
* Environmental impacts, costs, and comparisons with alternatives.
* The confidence range and the most important unresolved questions.

The project will remain willing to conclude that a proposed approach is impractical. Discovering a limit is part of discovering the truth.

9. Our North Star

We want scientific discovery to expand humanity’s options, protect the living world, and create opportunities for people to thrive.

The ultimate goal is not simply to capture more energy. It is to help build a civilization capable of using its knowledge wisely, sharing its benefits fairly, and treating the health of our planet as a fundamental priority.

The measure of an advanced civilization is not how much power it possesses, but how wisely and compassionately it uses that power.

Could the plasma and magnetic environments of the giant planets offer a pathway to useful electrical power?

We don’t know yet. That’s exactly why we’re investigating.

Plasmionyx is a proposed public-interest research initiative exploring the physics and engineering of possible energy extraction from Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune.

Our goal is to determine what is physically possible, what might be technologically achievable, and whether any proposed system could deliver meaningful net electrical power.

We believe ambitious questions deserve rigorous investigation, open collaboration, and a commitment to the public good.

What we’re working toward

We aim to:

* Compare the plasma and electromagnetic environments of all four giant planets.
* Investigate realistic energy-collection concepts, including electrodynamic tethers and electromagnetic induction.
* Model potential electrical output, engineering requirements, operating losses, and uncertainty.
* Identify the smallest practical experiments that could test key assumptions.
* Publish reproducible methods, calculations, and findings, including negative results.
* Explore the environmental and public-interest implications of any promising technology.

We are not asking anyone to accept a conclusion in advance. We are building a research effort to find out what the evidence supports.

Who we’d love to work with

We’re looking for people willing to contribute knowledge, curiosity, and thoughtful criticism.

* Plasma physicists and magnetospheric scientists to help establish the physical foundations.
* Planetary scientists to interpret spacecraft observations and compare the four planetary environments.
* Electrical and aerospace engineers to evaluate collection systems, materials, and operating constraints.
* Scientific programmers and numerical modelers to build transparent, reproducible simulations.
* Energy systems researchers to assess power conversion, losses, and realistic net output.
* Research integrity and governance advisers to help establish transparent, independent oversight.
* Educators, students, and public engagement volunteers to make the work accessible and invite broader participation.

You do not need to fill every role, have an institutional affiliation, or contribute money. A focused contribution of a few hours a month could help us get started.

How we’ll begin

Our first phase will rely on public spacecraft data, existing research, open-source software, and collaborative modeling. We do not need to purchase hardware before establishing whether the underlying concepts merit further investigation.

Our first major milestone is a reproducible feasibility assessment of Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune, reviewed by independent experts and shared publicly.

We’ll document our assumptions, distinguish estimates from demonstrated results, and be honest about what we do not yet know.

What makes this initiative different

Plasmionyx is founded on the belief that scientific progress should serve people and the planet.

We support:

* Open research and independent review.
* Transparent funding and decision-making.
* Honest reporting of uncertainty and unfavorable results.
* Environmental responsibility.
* Fair access to the benefits of successful discoveries.
* Collaboration across disciplines and national boundaries.

We welcome skeptics as much as enthusiasts. Critical questions strengthen a project when everyone is committed to following the evidence.

The bigger vision

Imagine a future in which energy innovation helps restore ecosystems, expands human opportunity, and gives communities more freedom to shape their lives.

That future will require more than new technology. It will require scientific honesty, responsible stewardship, and a commitment to ensuring that progress benefits humanity broadly.

Plasmionyx begins with a question about distant worlds, but our purpose is rooted here at home.

Join us

If you have relevant expertise, enjoy investigating difficult scientific questions, or want to help build an open and responsible research community, we would love to hear from you.

Tell us what you could contribute, what questions you think we should investigate first, or what assumptions you believe deserve the toughest scrutiny.

Let’s explore the possibilities together, without promising what the evidence has not yet established.
